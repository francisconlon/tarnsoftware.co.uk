Hole Crunch privacy policy

Effective 30 October 2026

This policy explains what information the game Hole Crunch (Android package uk.co.tarnsoftware.dropper) handles, why, and the choices you have. Hole Crunch is made by Tarn Software Ltd ("we", "us"), the data controller for the purposes of UK and EU data protection law. Contact us at info@tarnsoftware.co.uk.

The short version

  • You do not create an account. We do not run servers that store information about you.
  • Your progress, coins and settings are saved only on your device.
  • The game shows adverts through Unity's LevelPlay and Unity Ads. They use your device's advertising ID and other device information to show and measure adverts. Adverts are only personalised if you agree, and you can change your mind at any time in the game's settings.
  • To see how the game is played, it sends usage statistics (levels played, offers seen, purchases made) to Google Analytics for Firebase. They do not include your advertising ID and are not used for adverts.
  • Purchases are handled by Google Play. We never see your payment details.
  • Hole Crunch is not directed at children under 13.

Information saved on your device

The game saves your progress, coins, boosters, unlocked items, settings, your advertising choice, and a record of completed purchases (the store's order identifiers, so a purchase is never delivered twice) in the game's storage on your device. This information does not leave your device and is not sent to us. Uninstalling the game deletes it.

Advertising

Hole Crunch is free and is funded partly by adverts. Adverts are provided by LevelPlay, Unity's advert mediation service, and Unity Ads, both from Unity Technologies. When the game requests, shows or measures an advert, these services receive information such as:

  • your device's advertising ID (a resettable identifier provided by Android);
  • your IP address, and an approximate location derived from it;
  • device and app information (device model, operating system version, language, app version);
  • how you interact with adverts (for example views and taps).

They use it to deliver adverts, limit how often you see the same one, measure performance, and detect fraud.

Your choice. The first time you play, the game asks whether adverts may be personalised to you. If you say no, you still see adverts, but they are not targeted using your information. You can change your choice at any time in the game's settings. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android device settings (Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Settings > Google > Ads, depending on your device).

Unity's own handling of this information is described in the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

A one-time purchase in the game removes the adverts shown between levels. Adverts you choose to watch for a reward remain available.

Purchases

In-app purchases are optional and are processed by Google Play under Google's privacy policy. We never receive your card or payment details. To deliver what you bought, the game receives from Google Play the product and an order identifier, and keeps the order identifier on your device as described above. The purchase system is Unity In-App Purchasing, which may send technical information about purchases to Unity Technologies for diagnostics.

Usage statistics

To understand how the game is played and make it better (for example, which levels are too hard, and which offers players take up), Hole Crunch uses Google Analytics for Firebase, a Google service. It receives:

  • events from the game, such as starting, winning or losing a level, the offers you see and accept, and in-app purchases (the product and its price);
  • a random identifier for this installation of the game, which is not your name, your Google account or your advertising ID;
  • device and app information (device model, operating system version, language, app version), and an approximate location derived from your IP address;
  • when and for how long you play, so we can see how many players come back.

The game does not give Google Analytics your advertising ID, and the statistics are not used to choose or target adverts. We see them as reports. Google keeps the detailed statistics for up to 14 months; how Google handles information from apps that use its services is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Hole Crunch does not use a crash-reporting service of its own.

Why we can use your information

  • Consent, for personalised adverts. You can withdraw it at any time in the game's settings.
  • Legitimate interests, for showing non-personalised adverts that fund the free game, preventing fraud, and keeping the game working.
  • Legitimate interests, for usage statistics that show us how the game is played so we can improve it. If you object, contact us.
  • Contract, for delivering purchases you make.

How long information is kept

Information saved by the game stays on your device until you uninstall it. Information received by Unity or Google is kept according to their own policies, linked above; Google Analytics keeps detailed usage statistics for up to 14 months.

International transfers

Unity and Google may process information outside the UK and the European Economic Area. Where they do, they rely on safeguards recognised by UK and EU law, such as standard contractual clauses.

Children

Hole Crunch is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game and information about them has been collected, contact us and we will help.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete information about you, to object to or restrict its use, and to withdraw consent. Because we do not hold information about you on our own systems, most requests are best handled by changing your advertising choice in the game, resetting your advertising ID, or contacting Unity or Google directly. You can always contact us at info@tarnsoftware.co.uk, and we will help.

If you are in the UK, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). In the EU, you can complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and its effective date. Significant changes will also be noted in the game's release notes.
